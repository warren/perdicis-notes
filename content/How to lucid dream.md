# How to lucid dream
#publish 
_Related to [[Dreams]] (private page)._

![[Pasted image 20260621123744.png]]

Lucid dreaming = being awake during a dream. It is exactly as cool as it sounds and surprisingly low effort to learn! I taught myself in ~2 months with minimal work and you can too (if not faster).

LDing lets you run some nifty experiments on yourself from the comfort of your own bed. And it's just fun. Ever wondered what it's like to talk to your subconscious? Or return to your favorite place on earth? Or fly? Read on...

## Part 1/3: Remembering the dream
Peer-reviewed science ([including Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-025-00191-z)) finds that humans really do experience dreams every night -- we just forget them in the morning. So forgetfulness is the first obstacle you'll need to overcome as a dreamer. Learning to remember dreams takes a few weeks of mild effort but you should start seeing early results quickly. **All you need to do is write down your dreams first thing in the morning.**

![[Screenshot 2026-06-21 at 12.55.53 PM 1.png]]
_Using [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/) -- each outgoing edge represents a note_

Some people online call this a "keeping a dream journal." But I think that oversells how much effort is actually needed; my so-called "dream journal" was only a short collection of text notes on my phone, the majority of which were fire-and-forget notes -- written once and never reread. You could certainly do more than I did, but FYI the bare minimum is fine if you're not in a rush to "bulk up" this mental muscle. In general: know that to remember dreams, you'll need to rewire your brain's incentives so that it _wants_ to remember -- there's no reason to remember if you never use your dream memories for anything. The act of writing builds the remembrance muscle.

And, in case you're curious, the maintenance cost of remembrance is fairly low. I rarely document my dreams these days (only about once per month, and only for really spectacular dreams) and I recall ~25% of my dreams. When I was super into lucid dreaming, journaling daily, I recalled close to 100%. YMMV.

## Part 2/3: Triggering the dream
Having tried ~half a dozen methods of triggering lucidity, the one that worked for me was also the simplest: Finger Induced Lucid Dreaming (FILD).

FILD works by keeping your mind conscious while your body drifts off to sleep. To do this, you'll use a gentle repeated motion with your index and middle fingers.

Here's how to perform FILD (you can try these steps right now!):
1) Gently rest one hand, palm-down, flat on a surface.
2) Lightly press your index finger down, like you're playing piano.
3) Repeat the same motion with your middle finger.
4) OK, now repeat steps 2-3 -- except as lightly as you possibly can. You'll know you're doing it right if your fingers visually appear motionless. _This motion is what you'll use for FILD._

And -- here's how you use the motion to trigger a dream: at night, as you're getting into bed, position your hand somewhere comfortable on your mattress. Do the FILD motion, counting upwards from 0 to 150, incrementing +1 per finger press. After reaching 150, you may actually be dreaming and just not know it -- so do a reality check to find out! The simplest check I found was to plug my nose and try to breathe through it. If you can breathe through a plugged nose... you are definitely asleep. But you're still lucid! For now at least...

## Part 3/3: After entering the dream
Entering a dream like this is weirdly boring. Since nothing's happening, your mind will -- even after repeated practice -- often second guess you're even dreaming. "There's no way this bedroom is a dream," your sleepy brain typically tells itself. "I'd be able to tell. I should retry the FILD countdown. So all I need to do is reposition my hand and close my eyes..."

Push through that feeling! It doesn't make any sense! The sleeping mind often makes bizarre leaps of logic and you are not yet lucid if you're still experiencing these. The final step to achieve lucidity in my experience is **opening the bedroom door.**

> [!tip] Disclaimer: It gets pretty subjective from here
> Lucid dreams are different for everyone. But that's a boring answer to "what happens next?" so below I outline how my lucidity process goes, virtually every time. Just FYI your personal experience may vary.

Most of the time, walking to the bedroom door is easy; it feels the same as when you're awake. Uncommonly though, my brain will play adversarial tricks to prevent me from making it there. These tricks are usually one-offs (they happen once and never again -- maybe because I'd learn from them?) and have included fun things like:
- The covers are waaay heavier than usual, and I give up trying to lift them
- Halfway through my walk to the door, I become gripped by a paralyzing certainty that trying the door is a waste of time, and I'll miss out on a _real_ lucid dream unless I return to bed and retry FILD right now
- Each time I blink, I'm back in bed, so I need to reach the door without blinking

When you finally arrive at the door though, you're good. Universally what happens for me is that I open it -- and there's _nothing_ outside. Sometimes it's a white void, or a dark void, but the visual is dramatic enough to always snap my brain into lucidity. "Okay," my brain is seemingly saying, "you make a fair point. I'm definitely dreaming."

The white/black void quickly fills up. Usually after a few blinks or when I have a spot in mind, it'll morph into some place, usually a familiar one unless I had someplace specific in mind. The unconscious mind is fairly suggestible, so often just _thinking really hard_ about going somewhere will bring you there. For me, the default spot I like to gently suggest to my brain when I have nowhere specific is a familiar beach in New England at sunset, where I spent my childhood visiting and can easily picture from memory. In my dream, the beach's sky is painted impossibly deep shades of violet and crimson, reflected on glowing bands of golden sand.

What happens next: you can do almost anything! Flying is by far my favorite activity because it feels just as great as I imagined it would. Though maybe that's a self-fulfilling experience; I only enjoy it this much because I always expected I would, so my dreaming brain fabricates an experience perfectly tailored to my taste. If you have any anticipated experiences like that, you should try them and let me know how they feel!

### Experiments I've run / phenomena I've observed
#### Subconscious cause & effect
What's fascinating is I've never enjoyed _total_ control over my lucid dreams. A few sentences ago, I mentioned flying -- but I can't achieve this on command. Usually I'll need to fabricate at least 1 degree of indirection in order to fly. For examples:
- I conjure a set of Da Vinci-style mechanical wings, put them on, and use those to fly.
- I conjure a remote control to my hand with a single button on it -- pressing that button grants me the power of flight.

This consistent experience suggests there's an ingrained notion of cause-and-effect that needs fulfillment before my brain can suspend its disbelief. Weird (and neat!).

#### Object impermanence
In a few dreams, I've teleported myself to a library or a barbershop and read some of the literature lying around. Then, I looked away from whatever I'm reading. Upon looking back: I can tell the contents of the book/magazine have changed -- but I don't recall the original. That this behavior appears consistently in my lucid dreams suggests there's something deeper and weirder going on with (my?) human memory. A consistent explanation might be that the mind uses [fuzzy hashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzzy_hashing) of some kind to store unconscious memories?

#### NPCs
Characters in my dreams never have properly fleshed-out personalities. So conjuring up someone I know IRL would be virtually identical to conjuring up Abraham Lincoln -- different coats of paint, but same (or similar) underlying core personality.

This phenomenon is difficult to describe, but I'll try: you know how Nintendo Miis all look kinda similar because they draw from the same pool of facial features?

![[Mii.jpg]]

It's like that, except dream NPCs all draw from the same limited pool of personalities. Example personas:
- Really rushed, like they're late for something and don't have time to talk with you.
- Totally unbothered, and will universally go with the flow -- including being told they're a figment of my imagination.
- Hyperfocused on one particular thing, e.g. cooking if they happen to be in a kitchen, and it's near-impossible to convince them to do anything else.

I wonder if this limited personality pool is in some way a reflection of the limits of my unconscious psyche (because I'm familiar with those types of feelings!).

Disappointingly, this means I haven't had any illuminating, Yungian dialogues with my unconscious self, like some people describe online. My NPCs typically just agree with me.

Logically: this must mean I'm fully enlightened now and can learn nothing more from introspection. If any expensive therapy clinics in California would like to hire an incredibly self-actualized unlicensed therapist with no degree -- I offer very affordable rates; please reach out.

#### Lack of time dilation
AFAICT, Inception-style time dilation -- that 20 hours in the dream is 1 hour in real life -- is just totally made up (great movie though!). Time in dreams passes at the same rate as in the waking world. I've measured this by setting pretty aggressive timers in the waking world, and successfully achieved a 15 minute lucid dream that indeed felt like 15 minutes in the dream world.

## Improving your odds of LDing
Hopefully the above piques your interest enough to try lucid dreaming! Let me know how it goes.

If FILD doesn't end up working for you right off the bat, consider adding these gimmicks...

### Be as tired as possible (without ruining your sleep schedule)
Sorry, this is an unfair recommendation. You can't force tiredness to happen. More accurately what I mean is: set realistic expectations. You're not always going to be dead tired, and when you're not dead tired you're not going to slip into REM sleep (dreaming sleep) as smoothly.

For reference: REM sleep occurs at a few stages of the sleep cycle...

![[Stages-of-Sleep.jpg]]

... and for the FILD technique, we're targeting lucidity right at the very start, on the left side of "first cycle".

To rephrase _another_ way: if you're lazy and only want to attempt LDing when your odds are best to succeed: try on the nights when you're extra tired following a period of consistent bedtime.

And don't force yourself to be tired by sleeping super late. This steepens the difficulty of LDing by making your REM stage less predictable I usually fall asleep too quickly.

Getting tired through exercise might help though. But of course, you should be exercising anyway. Coming soon: Warren's guide on [[How to exercise]]

(Also, fun fact: waking during REM sleep typically causes you to remember dreams more often and generally have a smoother waking experience. You can do this by configuring a special REM-based alarm app using a smartwatch monitoring your heartbeat... I did this using "Sleep as Android," but I'm sure that 90% of my readers are iPhone users, so not worth documenting 🤠)

### Waking up in the middle of the night to induce tiredness
This technique is called WBTB ("Wake-Back-To-Bed"). I've found a little bit of success with it and would recommend.

What you do is: set an alarm for the middle of the night (give yourself like 4.5-5h of sleep), wake up for some short amount of time (5-30 min; you can play around with it), and then immediately reattempt FILD/some other lucidity technique as you're dozing off.

For me, WBTB also works especially well for short naps. In that scenario: set an alarm for ~30 minutes in the future, go to sleep while attempting FILD, wake up from your (hopefully lucid) dream, set another +30 min time, reattempt FILD, ... etc.. Even if you don't LD, simply remembering your naptime dreams is a good sign that you're making progress.

### Another technique: MILD
MILD = Mnemonic Induced Lucid Dreaming.

Unfortunately MILD didn't work for me- but it comes highly recommended so maybe it'll work for you. The idea is to train your brain to recognize when it's dreaming, thereby eliminating the primary issue with FILD: that it either works instantly or not at all. With MILD, you'll recognize you're dreaming _while in the dream_, effectively giving you infinite shots at inducing an LD.

The way MILD proponents explain their technique is basically by performing reality checks like half a dozen times per day in the waking world, to induce a state of gentle dream paranoia, where you're always aware of whether you're dreaming. It struck me as an awful lot of effort and awkward nose plugging, but check out [this Reddit post](https://www.reddit.com/r/LucidDreaming/comments/f43h14/how_to_master_the_mild_induction_method_to/) if you want to learn more.

## Misc notes that didn't find a place in this article
### Any fun stories?
My funniest one was the first time I achieved lucidity. I had just finished plugging my nose, breathing through it, and realizing I was asleep. "I wonder if I can make myself wake up?" I asked myself, in infinite wisdom. Yes. Yes, you can, and it happened instantly. 

The second time I achieved lucidity was sorta related to this. I had just finished watching The Ring. So, when I achieved lucidity, I found myself walking into a black void with nothing but a water well. I noped out of that dream super quickly.

Other fun stories involve enrolling in Hogwarts and zipping around on a broomstick

### The "Lightning" sensation when falling asleep
Usually, falling into lucidity is sensationless, but like 1/7 times I'll get a "zap" sensation running through my whole body. It feels like a warm wave of lightning. Not unpleasant, but definitely surprising.

### Any impact on sleep quality?
Nope! Go nuts!

### Did you say SEX
Yeah lucid dreaming a sexual fantasy is 100% possible. Fun, but IMO really weird when you think about it even just a little bit because you're basically Obsession (2026)-ifying an NPC into being your object of desire and that's inhuman/doesn't feel like consent/etc./etc./maybe I'm just projecting. But I'm too weirded out by it from experience to recommend. Go for it though; you're not hurting anyone.

### Sleep paralysis
I've only had it happen once in 5+ years. So I'm biased in saying it's not a problem but -- it's not really a problem. I agree it's freaky to be paralyzed in your own body, but that's basically what a rollercoaster is (btw: I hate rollercoasters) and it's over pretty quickly.

My one paralysis experience involved having a big heavy shadow dog sitting on my chest really close to my face. Spooky but only lasted ~15 seconds.

### Mood affecting the dream
TBH I can't tell. The vast majority of my lucid dreams occur when I'm in a fairly level headspace entering the night, so I've never had an angsty LD as the result of an angsty day, nor an exciting LD as the result of an exciting day.
